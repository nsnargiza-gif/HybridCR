Asymptotic analysis competing risks under hybrid censoring

Change the parameters and get the result. All computations (simulation, estimation by Newton's method, Fisher information, sample-size planning) run directly in the browser, based on the asymptotic theory from the dissertation. A tool for researchers working with incomplete time-to-event data.

▸ Everything is computed locally · exponential and Weibull models · two competing causes

▸ Assumptions and range of validity (important for correct interpretation)
The results rely on the conditions from the dissertation: (A1) smoothness of the cause intensities (three times differentiable in the parameter); (A2) the failure fraction β tends to a limit, the horizon τₙ→τ*<∞; (A3) the operational information is non-degenerate. The estimators are consistent and asymptotically normal under these conditions; the confidence intervals: asymptotic (accurate for large n). The latent cause times are assumed independent (module 05 shows the cost of its violation). For very small n or heavy censoring (β close to 0.2) the asymptotics are poorer: this information limit, the tool shows it honestly.

Simulation and estimation under hybrid censoring

Generate a sample from the two-cause model and see how the maximum-likelihood estimate recovers the true parameters. Move the failure fraction β, sample size n and the cause parameters, and the estimate is recomputed instantly. The data are generated by the hybrid scheme τₙ = min(X₍ᵣ₎, T₀): observation stops at the moment when r = ⌈βn⌉ failures have accrued, or the horizon T₀ is reached (whichever comes first).

02

Operational information and efficiency bound

Under censoring, efficiency is measured relative to operational information I = β·I_F, not the classical I_F. See how the limiting MLE variance and the Cramér–Rao bound change with the failure fraction β.

√n(â₁−a₁) → N(0, a₁λ/β); Var(â₁) ≈ a₁λ / (nβ)?Operational information: under censoring only a fraction β of failures is observed, so the available information equals β·I_F, not I_F. The variance bound is its inverse, hence the factor 1/β.

03

Newton's method: quadratic convergence, live

Run the MLE by Newton's method for the chosen model and watch how the error decreases quadratically: the number of correct digits doubles at each iteration. This is the core of the decision-support system.

04

Sample-size planner

Practical question: how many observations are needed to estimate the parameters with a given precision δ? Set the target precision and parameters, get the required n from the operational information.

n = maxⱼ [I⁻¹]ⱼⱼ / δ²?Where the formula comes from: asymptotically √n(θ̂ⱼ−θⱼ) ~ N(0, [I⁻¹]ⱼⱼ), so the standard error ≈ √([I⁻¹]ⱼⱼ/n). Setting it equal to the target δ and taking the maximum over coordinates gives the required n.

05

Effect of risk dependence on estimation

The standard assumption of independence of the competing causes is in practice untestable. If the causes are linked by a copula, the independence-based estimate departs systematically from the marginal rate. Move the dependence (Kendall's τ) and watch the discrepancy: it is linear in τ and small under weak dependence. Note: the dependence-discrepancy analysis is implemented for the exponential model (Clayton copula); for Weibull the copula model is more complex and is left to future work.

06

Power of the Wald test under a local alternative

Under the local alternative H₁ₙ: θ = θ₀ + n−1/2h the Wald statistic has non-central distribution χ²_q(λ). Via the absolute effect size Δ the non-centrality parameter equals λ = n·hᵀℐ(θ₀)h. Move the parameters and watch how the H₁ distribution 'creeps' onto H₀ under heavy censoring.

λ = n·hᵀℐ(θ₀)h?Non-centrality. Under H₁ₙ the Wald statistic ~ χ²_q(λ). Increasing n strengthens λ linearly; decreasing β (censoring) weakens it linearly. Note. λ is computed exactly as λ = n·hᵀℐ(θ₀)h, with ℐ the operational information of the two-cause Weibull model. For q>1 it depends on the direction of h, so for a fixed |h|=E its value lies between the smallest and largest eigenvalue of ℐ (reported as a range).
